# Historical boundaries of the playable campaign

The complete seven-chapter game is an original dramatic adaptation of the Grafton castaways' experience. Its historical route is researched; its puzzles, dialogue, portraits, locations as staged and individual gestures are fictional. This distinction also applies to lines labelled with a real person's name. No game line is presented as a quotation or a verbatim historical conversation.

## Evidence used for this pass

The web copy of chapter XI did not resolve during this check; the return passages were read in the acquired edition. Raynal’s evening-school and chess passages were read in the acquired 1874 English edition, printed pp. 159–160. The original French serial is registered separately. ## The people

The playable fifth survivor is **Alexander Maclaren/McLaren, called Alick**, not an invented sixth crewmate. The project uses “Alick McLaren” as its consistent display form and retains name variants in [CREW](../research/CREW.md). Raynal's account supplies some biographical descriptions; it is not a licence to invent a verified childhood, family history or diagnosis. Alick's inner voice and hands-on role in each puzzle are authored interpretation.

Musgrave's burden of command is dramatised without making him infallible. Raynal's technical skill does not make him the sole inventor of every useful idea. Henry's household work is not a racial, national or servant caricature, and cooking historically rotated. George's experience of waiting is not taken from a recovered personal diary. The story gives each person concerns and agency while keeping those imagined words separate from evidence.

The rescue includes people outside the five: Captain Cross and his household, people at Port Adventure, John Macpherson and supporters at Invercargill. Raynal records Māori among the people receiving them. The campaign credits this wider help without inventing named Indigenous biographies or treating the island's earlier human history as empty scenery.

## What is compressed or omitted

- **Time:** chapter transitions cover months, repeated repairs and setbacks. No recipe turns long tanning, boatbuilding or winter provisioning into a literal historical five-minute process.
- **Geography:** painted rooms are navigable dramatic compositions. They are not surveyed plans or an exact route map. Port Adventure and Invercargill remain named as different places.
- **Work:** materials and quantities are designed for legible inventory puzzles. Iron and steel are not described as inexhaustible or interchangeable historical resources; the failed drill exposes a limit.
- **Hazards:** no new graphic death, disputed cannibalism episode, illness diagnosis or species-specific hunting claim is invented to increase drama.
- **Dialogue:** every spoken line is original. Device speech is optional synthesis, not a recording of an actor or an historical voice.
- **Appearance:** generated paintings are original art, not authentic likenesses, archaeological reconstructions or imported assets from another adventure game.

## Invercauld and the smoke question

The groups occupied different parts of Auckland Island and did not meet during their marooning (E048). Invercauld's three survivors had already been rescued in May 1865. Musgrave's August smoke observation and the appendix's later speculation are chronologically problematic; they are not evidence of a secret encounter or a mission to find an Invercauld camp. The playable Grafton campaign does not turn that uncertainty into a discovery.

The different survival outcomes cannot be reduced to a controlled comparison of leadership. Equipment, access to the wreck, conditions and other circumstances differed. See [CONFLICTS](../research/CONFLICTS.md) and [FACT_INFERENCE_FICTION](../research/FACT_INFERENCE_FICTION.md).
