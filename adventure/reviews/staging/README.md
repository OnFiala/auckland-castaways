# Offline staging proof

`scene-scale-proof.webp` is a static Pillow composite. It combines original stage paintings with production target positions, prop atlas crops, and measured work-pose silhouettes. The six panels cover a first-chapter outdoor scene and later interior, forge, open boat, reunion and slipway scenes. Each shows a single plausible action instant plus the people exposed by the engine at the start of that scene; it does not exercise transitions, CSS animation, hit testing, lighting, or every state.

Recreate with `python adventure/reviews/staging/proof.py` from the repository root (Python with Pillow and Node). The script calls the actual engine, stage and prop registries; stage coordinates and height interpolation are not hand-entered into the sheet. It uses the measured work-pose ground anchors and the walking viewport's standing-height ratio. The host height is the scene's `heightAt` value: the actual upright painted silhouette is approximately 95–97% of that height, as in the calibrated viewport. Figures are ordered by foot depth, props by target order; CSS shadows, effects and overlays are omitted. The beach uses the older painting, the others use voyage paintings; this does not reconstruct the previous version's inset companion CSS.

Visual review after the hand-position revision: the selected rope, kettle, leather, bailer and pitch-pot actions read as near or touching the objects in the composite; the table action is an approximate gesture toward the furniture. Small objects and fingertip overlap are difficult to judge at contact-sheet size, and the sheet cannot establish exact contact or interaction timing in the running game. Adult proportions read plausibly against the cabin table, forge anvil and boat rail. The original 1672 × 941 paintings are resized in full to 840 × 473 panels without a deliberate crop; the tiny aspect-ratio difference introduces negligible stretching. Perspective, lens, furniture and body sizes are artistic constructions, not surveyed or measured reconstructions. The reunion panel deliberately shows Musgrave, Henry and George, rather than inventing a meeting between the two shipwreck parties.
